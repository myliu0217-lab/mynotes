Supplementary Fig. 7. Design and characterization of the programmable gain amplifier
(PGA). (a) Design of a ping-pong charge amplifier. This is the first stage of the PGA. This charge
amplifier moves the charge integrated inside the pixel to its transfer capacitors CT1 and CT2 in a
time-multiplxed manner. The ping-pong operation eliminates the need for two charge amplifiers,
thereby saving power. A single ended version is shown for clarity while the actual implementation
is differential. The operational amplifier (OPAMP) is implemented as the folded cascaded
operational transconductance amplifier (OTA) in (d). This OTA has cascaded current sources at
the second stage to boost DC gain. (b) Design of a programmable gain ADC driver. This is the
second stage of the PGA following the charge amplifier. Correlated double sampling is used to
enhance the linearity as described in 83. A single-ended version is shown for clarity and the OTA
is the same as (d) without the cascoding at the second stage. (c) The timing diagram of the switches
in (a) and (b). (d) The OTA design with common-mode feedback. (e) The simulated linearity of
the neural amplifier and the charge amplifier. Spurious free dynamic range (SFDR) exceeds 75
dB. (f) The simulated linearity of the PGA. SFDR exceeds 71 dB.
