Supplementary Fig. 16. Mechanical flexibility of thinned BISC chips. (a) Photograph of a
thinned chip (left) next to an unthinned, rigid chip (right). The thinned sample spontaneously
conforms to the surface of a glass beaker with a radius of curvature of ~30 mm using water as the
wetting liquid. (b) Photograph of a chip bent to a curvature of ~10 mm in the more robust direction
(front surface under tension) below the spontaneous elasto-capillary length cutoff. Kapton tape is
used to supply the necessary bending force. No fracturing occurs in the silicon down to this
curvature. (c) Bending down to 10 mm radius in the opposite direction results in brittle fracture,
where the silicon experiences greater tensile stresses due to the position of the neutral plane.
